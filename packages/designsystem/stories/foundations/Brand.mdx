# Brand

The apps built on this system are a **family**: Medier and Startside, both under lmfaole.party. Today they look the same, and like nothing in
particular. A brand makes each one recognisable and the family coherent, without taking anything from what the apps are for: your music, your
films, your morning. The rule of this page in one line: **the content leads, the brand signs**.

## 1. What the research says

How mature systems and well-known brands handle a brand across a family of products, condensed. The sources are linked; the principles in
section 2 follow from them.

**Brand is a layer, not a second system.** Primer keeps the product's functional tokens apart from a separate brand system for marketing
([Primer colour usage](https://primer.style/product/getting-started/foundations/color-usage/), [Primer Brand](https://primer.style/brand/));
Spotify's Encore is one foundation with layers of local systems for each product above it
([Spotify Engineering](https://engineering.atspotify.com/2023/05/multiple-layers-of-abstraction-in-design-systems)). Carbon's themes change
values and never roles ([Carbon themes](https://carbondesignsystem.com/elements/themes/overview/)). Digdir's designsystemet and NAV's Aksel
theme through a small set of colour tokens set by an attribute, and **status colours never inherit a brand**
([designsystemet colours](https://www.designsystemet.no/no/fundamentals/code/colors), [Aksel theming](https://aksel.nav.no/grunnleggende/styling/theming)).
Radix Themes gives one accent per app from a closed set of tested scales ([Radix colour](https://www.radix-ui.com/themes/docs/theme/color)).

**One accent, used sparingly, and the content first.** Apple's guidelines: use the accent colour judiciously, defer to the content, do not
put the logo everywhere, keep branding out of the launch screen, and let onboarding be where the app introduces itself
([Apple HIG, Branding](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/branding)). Geist and Polaris keep their interfaces
near-neutral and reserve colour for meaning ([Geist colours](https://vercel.com/geist/colors), [Polaris colours](https://polaris.shopify.com/design/colors));
Atlassian separates the brand's expressive palette from the product's functional one ([Atlassian colour](https://atlassian.design/foundations/color),
[Atlassian brand](https://atlassian.design/brand)).

**Content can carry the colour.** Material You derives the palette from the person's wallpaper
([Making Material You](https://design.google/library/making-material-you), [Android dynamic colour](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/views/theming/dynamic-colors));
Collins' 2015 identity for Spotify let duotone and the artists' own pictures be the brand ([Collins, Spotify](https://www.wearecollins.com/work/spotify)).
Here the `AmbientBackdrop` and the light take their colour from a cover, so the app's hue stays out of the way.

**Brand strategy comes before the visuals.** A brand platform (purpose, values, personality) and a position decide the verbal and visual
identity, not the other way round. Mailchimp keeps one **voice** and varies the **tone** with the moment
([Mailchimp voice and tone](https://styleguide.mailchimp.com/voice-and-tone/), [NN/g tone of voice](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/tone-of-voice-dimensions/)).
Airbnb's Bélo was drawn from «belong anywhere» (Brand New on the [Airbnb identity](https://www.underconsideration.com/brandnew/archives/new_logo_and_identity_for_airbnb_by_designstudio.php)).
Vipps is a Norwegian example of a brand built on one plain promise and a strong colour and a recognisable voice ([Vipps brand](https://brand.vipps.no/)); NRK
is a masterbrand over many channels and services ([NRK](https://www.nrk.no/informasjon/)).

**Architecture: branded house, house of brands, endorsed.** Apple and Google are branded houses: products named plainly under one master
brand, with a systematic family of product icons ([Evolving the Google identity](https://design.google/library/evolving-google-identity)).
Uber's identity was built as one system across a family of services ([Uber identity](https://www.underconsideration.com/brandnew/archives/new_logo_and_identity_for_uber_done_in_house.php)).
An **endorsed** brand lets each product have its own name and mark, with the family vouching for it quietly
([Brand architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brand_architecture)). It fits two small apps with distinct purposes under one domain.

**Distinctive assets are built by repetition.** A brand is recognised by a few assets (a colour, a shape, a sound, a motion, a recurring
moment) used the same way for a long time ([Ehrenberg-Bass Institute](https://www.ehrenbergbass.com/)). Netflix's «N» and its «tudum» are
one mark across picture, motion and sound ([Netflix brand](https://brand.netflix.com/en/)). Spotify Wrapped is a recurring **signature
moment**: a yearly, personal story the brand owns ([Wrapped 2023](https://newsroom.spotify.com/2023-11-29/wrapped-user-experience-2023/)).
Niche media apps earn love through personality: Letterboxd's three dots ([Letterboxd brand](https://letterboxd.com/about/brand/)), Last.fm's
scrobble and its red ([Last.fm](https://www.last.fm/about)).

**Icons are part of the brand.** A maskable icon keeps its drawing inside a safe circle ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/maskable-icon)); a
handful of files (an SVG favicon, a PNG fallback, an apple-touch-icon, the manifest's icons) cover the platforms
([Evil Martians](https://evilmartians.com/chronicles/how-to-favicon-in-2021-six-files-that-fit-most-needs)). `theme-color` tints the
browser around the page ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/HTML/Reference/Elements/meta/name/theme-color)). A mark that
carries meaning needs 3:1 ([WCAG 1.4.11](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#non-text-contrast)).

## 2. Principles

1. **The content leads, the brand signs.** Covers, posters, weather and the day are the colour of a page. The brand is a signature in the
   chrome, never a coat of paint over the content.
2. **One hue per app, from a closed and tested set.** The hue is identity, never meaning: not success, not a warning, not a link, not a series.
3. **One mark per app, one shape for the family.** Every app is a glyph on the same rounded tile; the hue and the glyph tell them apart.
4. **The family endorses, it does not lead.** «Medier · lmfaole»: the app's name first, the family after it and quieter.
5. **Voice is the strongest asset we have.** Plain Norwegian, the same in every app; the tone moves with the moment.
6. **Signature moments, neutral everyday.** The brand shows where the app meets you (the first run, the mark, an empty page), not on every
   control.
7. **Same assets, used the same way, for a long time.** A new hue or glyph is a decision for the family, made here.
8. **Neutral by default.** Without `data-brand`, every token is the ink: an app is branded when it chooses to be.

## 3. The layers

| Layer | What it is | Where it lives |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Strategy | The brand platform: purpose, personality, voice, distinctive assets (section 5) | This page; the app's README |
| Brand tokens | `--brand`, `--brand-foreground`, `--brand-subtle`, one set per hue, `light-dark()` | `theme.css`, `[data-brand="…"]` |
| Role tokens | `background`, `foreground`, `primary`, `ring`, `destructive`, `series-*`… unchanged; `ring` and the primary `Button` take the brand when one is set | `theme.css` |
| Components | `AppMark`, `Wordmark`, `NavBar` (`glyph`, `family`), `FirstRun` (`glyph`) | `src/components` |
| Assets | The icons and favicon from the same drawing as `AppMark` | `npx designsystem-app-icons` |

The hues are named for their colour (`rose`, `amber`, `teal`, `blue`, `violet`), not for an app: the design system knows nothing about the
apps (README). There is no green: green reads as success.

## 4. Where the brand goes, and where it never goes

| The brand is here | The brand is never here |
| --- | --- |
| The app's mark (`AppMark`, `Wordmark`) | Text in the content, headings, links in running text |
| The current place in the `NavBar`: its icon in the hue on a tint of it | Charts (`series-*`, `chart-*` and the grey defaults stay as they are) |
| The focus ring (`ring`; the brand has 4.5:1, a ring needs 3:1) | A state: `destructive`, a `Badge`, `StateSteps`, events, `transit-*` |
| The one primary `Button` on a page, a ticked checkbox or radio | Backgrounds of cards, sections, the page, a `PageHeader` |
| The first run's header (`FirstRun` `glyph`) | Over a cover or a poster; the `AmbientBackdrop`, which is the content's colour |
| The app's icons and favicon | `theme-color` and the manifest's colours, for now (an open question) |

## 5. The brand platform

A brand platform is a page of words that decides the rest. Fill it in for each app, keep it in the app's README, and change it rarely.

| Part | Question | Length |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Purpose | Why does the app exist, for the person who uses it? | One sentence |
| Position | For whom, instead of what, and what makes it different? | One sentence |
| Personality | Three adjectives, each with what it is **not** («warm, not cute») | Three pairs |
| Voice | How it speaks always; how the tone moves (welcome, empty, error, success) | Three lines and four examples |
| Distinctive assets | Hue, glyph, name, a recurring moment, a motion | A list |
| Signature moments | Where the brand is felt: first run, an empty page, a yearly or weekly look back | Two or three |
| Do / don't | Rules a reviewer can check | Five each |

### The family's platform

> **Approved** (November 2026) and in use in both apps: Medier in `amber` with `play`, Startside in `teal` with `sunrise`. The draft
> proposed `violet` for Medier; the owner chose amber.

**lmfaole (the family)**

- **Purpose:** small, personal tools for one person's day and taste, made with care and without noise.
- **Architecture:** endorsed. Each app has its own name, hue and glyph; «lmfaole» follows quietly in the `Wordmark` and the footer.
- **Personality:** personal, not private; careful, not precious; plain, not dull.
- **Voice:** plain Norwegian (bokmål), short sentences, the person addressed as «du», no exclamation marks, no jargon. Dry humour at most, and
  never in an error.
- **Distinctive assets:** the rounded tile with one white glyph; Atkinson Hyperlegible as the type; one light for the page; one hue per app.
- **Don't:** a slogan, a mascot, a gradient brand colour, the family's name before the app's.

**Medier**

- **Purpose:** see what you have listened to and watched, and find your way back to it.
- **Position:** for one person who scrobbles and logs, instead of four services' own pages: one calm place where the covers lead.
- **Personality:** warm, not cute; curious, not nosy; quiet, not cold.
- **Hue and glyph:** `amber` (warm, the glow of a record player and a cinema; away from `destructive` red and Last.fm's red; the draft proposed `violet`, the approved platform chose amber), `play`.
- **Voice examples:** welcome «Her er det du har hørt og sett.»; empty «Ingenting spilt ennå i dag.»; error «Fikk ikke kontakt med Last.fm.
  Prøver igjen om litt.»; success «Koblet til Trakt.»
- **Signature moments:** the first run; the album page's sleeve in the light; a look back at the week or the year (a «Wrapped» of your own,
  later).
- **Do:** let the cover's colour lead (`AmbientBackdrop`); keep the mark in the rail. **Don't:** tint covers, charts or a source's logo
  amber.

**Startside**

- **Purpose:** the morning in one look: what to do, what the weather is, when to leave.
- **Position:** for a developer starting the day, instead of five tabs: calm, brisk and useful before coffee.
- **Personality:** calm, not sleepy; brisk, not curt; practical, not bare.
- **Hue and glyph:** `teal` (morning and clear; `amber` is the late departure and the warning event, `blue` the tram and the cold), `sunrise`.
- **Voice examples:** welcome «God morgen. Her er dagen din.»; empty «Ingen pull requests venter på deg.»; error «Fikk ikke hentet
  avgangene. Viser de siste vi hadde.»; success «Lagret.»
- **Signature moments:** the first run; the greeting and the weather stage at the top of the morning.
- **Do:** keep transit, weather and GitHub states in their own colours. **Don't:** use teal for a line, a temperature or a check.

## 6. Distinctive assets, and keeping them

- **Colour:** the app's hue, in the places in section 4 and nowhere else. A brand is not made stronger by more of it.
- **Shape:** the rounded tile (`rx 22` on a 100 grid), the same for every app, with one glyph. The tile in the page (`AppMark`) and on the
  home screen are drawn from the same data (`src/lib/app-glyphs.json`).
- **Type:** Atkinson Hyperlegible: legible first, and it is the family's voice on the page.
- **Motion:** the light that follows the pointer is still the family's one moving signature, and every app moves on the same productive
  tokens. On top of that, each app has an **accent** for its signature moments only, set by its hue («Motion», section 9). Medier (`amber`)
  is rhythmic: the play mark beats twice on the light spring. Start (`teal`) is calm and rising: the sun clears its horizon, with no
  overshoot. The accent plays in the first run (`AppMark reveal`), as it ends (`FirstRunFinish`), on an app's first empty page
  (`EmptyState reason="first-run"`, its «+» in the hue) and in a long wait (`AppMark loading`). Each plays once, within a second, and not at all under reduced motion.
- **Words:** the name, said the same way in the `<title>`, the manifest, the `Wordmark` and the first run.
- **Consistency over time:** change a hue or a glyph only with a new entry on this page and in the README; never per page or per season.

## 7. Using it

Set the hue once, on the root of the app, and give the `NavBar` the app's glyph and family:

```tsx
<html lang="nb" {...htmlAttributes(prefs)} data-brand="amber">
  …
  <NavBar label="Hovedmeny" title="Medier" glyph="play" family="lmfaole" items={items} />
```

`brandAttributes("amber")` from `lib/brand` gives the attribute with its type. The first run takes the same glyph: `<FirstRun glyph="play" … />`.
The hue brings the app's motion accent with it («Motion», section 9). For the current place to slide between pages, add
`transitionAttributes()` from `lib/motion` beside it.

Draw the icons from the same mark, in the app's folder:

```sh
npx designsystem-app-icons --hue amber --glyph play
npx designsystem-app-icons --hue teal --glyph sunrise --apple app/apple-icon.png --favicon app/icon.png --svg app/icon.svg
```

It writes `favicon.svg`, a 64 px PNG, a 180 px apple-touch-icon, and 192, 512 and maskable 512 icons, and prints the manifest's colours.
Keep `theme-color` and the manifest's `background_color` the page's (`#ffffff` light, `#0a0a0a` dark): the browser's chrome is not the brand's
place, the icon is.

## 8. Do and don't

| Do | Don't |
| --- | --- |
| Set `data-brand` once, on `<html>` | Set it on a section to colour it, or mix two hues on a page |
| Use the brand through the components | Write `text-brand`, `bg-brand` in an app (`className` is not an app's) |
| Keep the app's name the ink, the mark in the hue | Colour the name, a heading or a link with the brand |
| Let a cover, a poster or the weather be the colour of a page | Tint a picture, a chart or a status with the brand |
| Say the app's name the same way everywhere | Add a slogan, a mascot or a second logo |
| Choose a new hue or glyph here, for the family | Invent one in an app |
