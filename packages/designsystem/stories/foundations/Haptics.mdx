# Haptics

A haptic is the third channel of feedback, after what is seen and what is heard. A short, clear tap confirms what a hand just did. It
**never carries meaning on its own**: every buzz goes with words, a symbol or a position on the screen. Haptics are opt-in for each control,
progressive (they do nothing where they cannot work), and the reader can turn them off. The research behind this guide is in its sources.

## 1. Principles

1. **Confirm a gesture, never start one.** A buzz answers a tap, a click, a change or a key, once the outcome is known. Never on hover, scroll,
   focus, page load, a timer or a live update.
2. **Few and semantic.** There are seven kinds, each with one meaning everywhere (Apple: use the system patterns for what they mean,
   consistently). An app never writes its own milliseconds.
3. **Clear, not buzzy.** Short taps (10–30 ms), every pattern under 200 ms. On the web there is no amplitude, so «soft» comes from short
   (Android: given a choice between buzzy haptics and none, choose none).
4. **Rare.** Apple: «Avoid overusing haptics». If the reader feels it often, take it away. Not every button buzzes.
5. **Never alone.** The visible state, a `Banner`, the error under a field or a live region says it too (Apple: haptics complement, they do
   not replace).
6. **The reader decides.** «Make haptics optional» (Apple). «Vibrasjon» in `AppearanceSettings` is the off switch. Without a choice, `prefers-reduced-motion`
   is a hint: only the outcomes are felt.

## 2. Vocabulary

`haptic(kind)` from `lib/haptics`, each kind a `navigator.vibrate` pattern of on and off milliseconds:

| Kind | Pattern (ms) | Like | Use it for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `selection` | `10` | iOS selection, Android `CLOCK_TICK` and `SEGMENT_TICK` | A value changed: a switch, a choice, a step forward, a row moved past another |
| `impact-light` | `15` | iOS impact light | Something small snapped into place |
| `impact-medium` | `25` | iOS impact medium, Android `GESTURE_END` | Something larger landed: a pull to refresh released |
| `success` | `15 · 60 · 25` | iOS notification success, Android `CONFIRM` | It worked: two taps, the second firmer |
| `warning` | `25 · 90 · 25` | iOS notification warning | A destructive confirm, or done with a caveat |
| `error` | `30 · 50 · 30 · 50 · 30` | iOS notification error, Android `REJECT` | It did not work: three firm taps |
| `signature` | by `data-brand` | Apple Pay's «two simple taps» | The moment an app is set up (the end of `FirstRun`), and nothing else |

### The brand's signature

| `data-brand` | App | Pattern (ms) | Feels like |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `amber` | Medier | `12 · 70 · 40` | «play»: a tap, then a push, short then long, like ▶ starting |
| `teal` | Start | `6 · 40 · 10 · 40 · 16 · 40 · 24` | a sunrise: four taps that grow |
| any other, or none | | as `success` | |

A signature works through restraint: one moment, always with the moment that is seen (the completion of the first run). If it were used
everywhere, it would be noise. Under reduced motion it is played as a plain `success`.

## 3. Where the design system buzzes

| Component | Kind | When |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `FirstRun` | `selection` · `error` · `signature` | A step forward, a step that fails its checks, and «Ferdig» once it is sent |
| `ReorderList` | `selection` | A row moved with its arrows, or dragged past another |
| `CheckboxList`, `ToggleMatrix`, `ChoiceCards` | `selection` (`data-haptic`) | A box ticked or a card chosen, once the app has `Haptics` |
| «Vibrasjon» (`AppearanceSettings`) | `selection` | Turning vibration on, so the reader feels what they chose |

`TiltButton` has none on purpose: it is only shown on iOS, where the tilt permission is asked for, and iOS has no `vibrate`.

## 4. In an app

1. **Once, in the layout:** `<Haptics />` (Next.js) or, in Astro, `<script>import { listenForHaptics } from "@lmfaole/designsystem/lib/haptics";
   listenForHaptics()</script>`. This plays the controls that are marked `data-haptic="<kind>"`, also in server-drawn markup.
2. **On the settings page:** «Vibrasjon» comes with `AppearanceSettings`, in its «Effekter» section after the effects themselves. It applies at once and is kept in this browser.
3. **Mark sparingly:** `data-haptic="warning"` on the button that confirms something destructive. In a script, call `haptic("success")`
   after a save that is confirmed on screen.

```tsx
import { Haptics } from "@lmfaole/designsystem/components/haptics"
import { AppearanceSettings } from "@lmfaole/designsystem/components/appearance-settings"
import { haptic } from "@lmfaole/designsystem/lib/haptics"

<Button type="submit" variant="outline" data-haptic="warning">Slett listen</Button>
```

### Off, and who decides

- **The reader:** `localStorage` `ds-haptics` = `on` or `off`, written by «Vibrasjon» in `AppearanceSettings`. It is kept per device, not per account, because
  it is the phone that vibrates.
- **The page:** `data-haptics="off"` on `<html>`, or on any element above a control. The nearest one wins, and it beats the reader's choice.
  Use it for a preview or a kiosk.
- **The hint:** under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`, with no choice of the reader's own, the ticks and impacts are not played, and the
  signature is a plain `success`. An explicit «on» gives everything.
- **The throttle:** a buzz within the previous pattern plus 80 ms is dropped, unless it says more (an error cuts in over a tick). A fast run
  of changes is never a storm.

## 5. What each platform can do

| Where | What is felt |
| --- | --- |
| Chrome, Samsung Internet and WebView on Android, and installed PWAs from them | Everything, after the first tap or key (sticky user activation). Not in cross-origin iframes. |
| Firefox on Android | Nothing: `vibrate` is disabled and answers `true` anyway |
| Firefox on desktop | Nothing (removed in 129) |
| Safari, every browser on iOS, home-screen web apps on iOS | Nothing from `vibrate`. A native `<input type="checkbox" switch>` gives the system's own tap when the reader toggles it (iOS 18 and later), so «Vibrasjon» is one. |
| Desktop | Nothing; nothing is lost |

### Why not the iOS «hidden switch» trick

Some libraries fake an iPhone haptic by clicking a hidden `<label>` for a switch from a script. We do not use it:

- WebKit now requires a trusted event and user activation for the switch's haptic, so a scripted click no longer works (iOS 26.5).
- The version that still works lays an invisible control over the button. That changes hit-testing and focus, and adds a switch to the
  accessibility tree that the reader never asked for.
- It gives one fixed tap, so `success` and `error` would feel the same.

Where a control *is* on or off, make it a real native `switch`, and the iPhone's own tap comes honestly.

A web haptics API (WICG «Web Haptics»: `navigator.playHaptics()` and a CSS `haptic` property, with semantic effects much like these) is
still an idea. When it ships, `haptic()` can use it underneath, and nothing in an app changes.

## 6. Accessibility

- **Never the only carrier.** WCAG has no criterion that names haptics. SC 1.3.3 Sensory Characteristics and SC 1.4.1 Use of Color state
  the same principle for other senses: one sense alone never carries meaning. Many readers feel nothing at all: a phone on the table,
  Safari, haptics off.
- **No OS setting reaches the web.** iOS System Haptics and Android's touch feedback are invisible to a page, and there is no media query.
  So the in-app switch is required, not optional.
- **Short and rare,** for people with sensory processing differences or vestibular disorders, and for the battery.
- **SC 2.2.2 Pause, Stop, Hide** is about content that moves or updates by itself. A brief answer to the reader's own gesture is outside it.
  Never vibrate on load or on a timer.

## 7. Do and don't

| Do | Don't |
| --- | --- |
| Buzz once the outcome is known, with what is seen | Buzz on hover, scroll, focus, load, a timer or a live update |
| Use a kind for its meaning | Write your own milliseconds, or make a long pattern |
| `selection` for a value changing | Give every button a buzz, or buzz on navigation |
| `error` with the error under the field | Let a rhythm be the only way to tell success from error |
| The signature at the end of the first run | The signature for routine actions |
| A native `switch` for an on/off setting | Fake iOS haptics with hidden or overlaid controls |
| «Vibrasjon» (`AppearanceSettings`) on the settings page | Turn it on again for a reader who turned it off |
