# Forms

Forms are rare in a read-mostly app, and so they are small: one column, a few fields, native controls. The rules are from GOV.UK
(validation, error summary, date input), Carbon (forms pattern), Atlassian (form) and Primer (toggle switch), put into our terms.

## 1. One column, label above

<TextField label="E-postadresse" name="epost" hint="For eksempel ola@eksempel.no." />

- The label is above the field and always visible. A placeholder is never the label.
- The hint says the format and stays visible; so does the error. There is no tooltip.
- Mark only the minority: «(valgfritt)» when most fields are required.
- Read-only is shown as text in a `FactList`. Avoid `disabled`: say why the value cannot be changed instead.

## 2. Validate when the form is sent

On submit, not while typing (GOV.UK). Carbon validates when the reader leaves a field; we follow GOV.UK because it keeps one rule for the whole form. Validate on the server as well. Do not use the HTML `required` attribute: use `novalidate` and say what is
needed in words.

When there are errors:

1. Show a `FormErrorSummary` at the top of the page and move focus to it.
2. List every error as a link to its field, in the same words as beside the field.
3. Keep what the reader wrote in the fields.
4. Start the page title with «Feil:».

<FormErrorSummary errors={[{ field: "epost", message: "Skriv en e-postadresse med @." }]} />

## 3. Words

Say what is wrong and how to fix it: «Skriv en e-postadresse med @, for eksempel ola@eksempel.no.» Never «Vennligst», «Beklager», «Ups»,
«ugyldig» or «du glemte». An error that is not about a field (no access, the service is down) is not a field error: it is a `Banner` or
an `ErrorMessage` for the part that failed.

## 4. Search and choice

<SearchField label="Søk i pull requests" action="#" />

A search is a GET form that goes to the same page with `?q=`, so the result has an address. It stands above the list with its label
visible, not behind an icon. Switching between views of the same page (a day, a line, a period) is `FilterChips`: up to six
chips, and beyond six it becomes a `NativeSelect` that goes to the chosen view by itself, so the app never chooses between the two. A
known list in a form is a `NativeSelect`.

One of a few values in a form is `ChoiceCards`: a card per value, a word and a sentence, the whole card the label. Several of them are
a `CheckboxList`.

<ChoiceCards name="start" legend="Hva vil du se først?" value="weather" options={[{ value: "weather", label: "Været", icon: "weather" }, { value: "transport", label: "Avganger", icon: "bus" }]} /> A calendar popover and an
autocomplete draw a menu in the page and are not part of the system; a date is a native date field, or three fields (day, month, year) when the reader knows it by heart.

## 5. Saving

A switch takes effect at once and the label names the setting, not the state. A checkbox needs an explicit save. Use one save pattern in a form.
The button names the verb («Lagre», «Opprett»). After saving, say it in the page («Lagret», a `Banner` or a line next to the form),
never in a toast, and take the reader to what they made.

While the server answers, show that it works: the save button is a `PendingButton` with `pendingLabel` in the same verb («Lagrer …», «Sjekker brukernavnet …»), and the layout runs `listenForPendingForms` (`PendingForms` in React) once. It swaps the label and adds a spinner at once, says it in a live region, and keeps a second press from sending the form twice. Never disable the button before it is pressed, and never leave it busy: the next page, or the error beside the field, ends the wait.

## 6. Settings

A settings page («Innstillinger») is the same in every app, and most of it is the design system's:

| Part | Component | The app passes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The overview: one row per subpage, with what is chosen there now | `SubPages` with `appearancePage(href, prefs)` and `appPage(href, appName)` (`lib/settings-pages`) first, then the app's own rows | the rows' `href`, the app's own pages |
| «Utseende»: theme, effects (with «Vibrasjon») and the light, as pictures, and «Lagre» | `AppearanceSettings` | `prefs`, `action`, `back` |
| «App»: installing the app | `InstallSettings` | `appName`, `cookiePath` |
| «Introen»: «Vis introen igjen» | `IntroSettings` | `action`, `back` |
| «Kilder»: where the data comes from | `SourcesSettings` | `sources` |
| A setting of the app's own | `SettingsSection` with the app's fields, in a form with `back` and a `PendingButton` «Lagre» | everything |

The words, the order and the section ids (`tema`, `effekter`, `lys`, `installer`, `intro`, `kilder`) are fixed, so a link to
`/innstillinger/utseende#lys` works in every app. The server action, the `prefs` and `seen` cookies and the field names (`theme`,
`effects`, `tilt`, `back`, `rerun`) stay the app's. A first run asks the same as «Utseende», one step at a time: `ThemeChoice`,
`EffectsChoice`, `TiltChoice`.

After a save the endpoint sends the reader back to `back` with `?lagret=1`, and the subpage shows `Banner tone="success"` («Lagret»)
under its title.
