# Presentation design

Apply these rules when building or reviewing slides. Check a deck against the checklist at the end before delivering. Deviate only with a reason.

## One idea per slide
- One message per slide. Title = the takeaway as a full sentence ("Salget økte 12 % i Q3"), not a topic ("Salg Q3").
- Max ~30 words of body text (excluding charts). Max 3–5 bullets, each ≤ 1–2 lines. If more is needed, split the slide.
- Build structure: opening/agenda → sections (divider slides) → content → summary/next steps. Aim at ~1–2 minutes per content slide.
- Slides support the speaker; detail belongs in speaker notes or a handout.
- Use the slide master/layouts (title, content, two-column, section, image) instead of manual placement; use real placeholders so outline view and accessibility work.

## Typography
- Sizes (16:9, 13.33×7.5 in): title 32–44 pt, body 24–28 pt, secondary/captions 18–20 pt. Never below 18 pt for projected content; footnotes/sources ≥ 12 pt. Chart labels ≥ 14 pt.
- Max 2 font families (one heading + one body, or a single family with weights). Max ~3 sizes per slide.
- Pairing: sans-serif for both is safest on screen (e.g. Calibri/Segoe UI/Arial, Inter, Source Sans). Serif heading + sans body (e.g. Georgia + Calibri) for a editorial feel. Use fonts available on the presenting machine, or embed them.
- Left-align text (centre only short titles/quotes). Line spacing 1.0–1.2 for titles, 1.1–1.3 for body. Line length ≤ ~60 characters in body text.
- Hierarchy by size and weight (bold/regular), not by underline, ALL CAPS, or many colors. Avoid italics for long text.
- Do not shrink text to fit (disable autofit shrinking); cut words instead.

## Color and contrast
- Palette: 1 neutral background + 1 text color + 1 primary accent + 1 secondary accent (60/30/10 rule). Max ~5 colors total.
- Contrast ratio text/background ≥ 4.5:1 (≥ 3:1 for large text, i.e. ≥ 18 pt or bold ≥ 14 pt, and for meaningful graphics) per WCAG 2.x. Prefer ≥ 7:1 for projected slides.
- Dark text on light background for bright rooms/print; light on dark for dark rooms. Don't mix per-slide randomly. Use off-white/near-black (#FAFAFA / #1A1A1A) rather than pure extremes to reduce glare.
- Never rely on color alone (≈ 8 % of men are red–green colour blind): add labels, shapes or patterns. Avoid red/green pairs; use blue/orange.
- Accent color highlights the one thing that matters; keep the rest gray.
- Avoid text over busy images without a ≥ 50–60 % opaque overlay or solid panel.
- Check on a projector-like setting: pale grays and yellows on white often disappear.

## Layout, grid and whitespace
- Margins ≥ 0.5 in (about 5 % of width) on all sides; ~0.75 in is comfortable. Keep titles at the same position on every slide.
- Use a grid: 12 columns (or 2/3 columns) with consistent gutters (~0.25–0.3 in). Align edges of text, images and shapes to the grid; use PowerPoint's Align/Distribute and guides.
- Common compositions: full-bleed image + text panel; 1/3 text + 2/3 visual; two equal columns for comparison. Rule of thirds for focal points.
- Whitespace: aim for ≥ 40 % of the slide empty. Group related items closely and separate groups with larger gaps (proximity). Use equal spacing steps (e.g. 0.125/0.25/0.5 in).
- Reading order: top-left → bottom-right (Z/F pattern). Put the key item where the eye lands first.
- Consistent elements: same logo position, footer, slide number, bullet style, and corner radius throughout. Logo small (≤ ~1 in high) and not on every slide if it distracts.

## Charts and data visualisation
- Choose by question: comparison → bar/column; trend over time → line; part-to-whole → stacked bar or (≤ 4 slices) pie/donut; relationship → scatter; distribution → histogram/box. Avoid 3D, shadows, secondary axes, and radar charts.
- Title the chart with the insight; label directly on the data (end of line, bar values) instead of legends where possible.
- Bar charts start at zero. Sort bars by value unless categories are ordered (time, scale). Limit to ≤ 5–7 categories, ≤ 4 lines.
- Remove chartjunk: heavy gridlines, borders, redundant axis titles. Light-gray (#D9D9D9) thin gridlines at most.
- Gray for context, one accent color for the highlighted series. Same color = same meaning across all slides.
- Tables: ≤ 5 columns × 6 rows on a slide, right-align numbers, consistent decimals, highlight the key cell. Use native charts/tables (editable, accessible), not screenshots.
- Always cite source and period under the chart (≥ 12 pt, gray).
- Numbers: round for projection (12 %, not 11,837 %).

## Imagery and icons
- Use one high-quality image per message; photos ≥ 1920 px wide for full-bleed (≈ 150 dpi at 13.33 in). Never stretch: keep aspect ratio and crop instead.
- Prefer authentic, relevant photos over generic stock clip art. No clip art, no WordArt, no low-res screenshots.
- Icons: one consistent set (same stroke weight, style, color). Diagrams: ≤ ~7 elements, simple shapes, consistent arrows.
- Screenshots: crop to the relevant part, add a highlight (box/arrow) and enlarge until legible.
- Add alt text to meaningful images; mark decorative ones as decorative. Respect licenses; credit images (small caption, ≥ 10–12 pt) .
- Keep file size manageable: compress pictures (Picture Format → Compress, 150–220 ppi).
- Animation: minimal; use for progressive reveal only (fade/appear, < 0.5 s). No spinning/bouncing. Avoid autoplay media unless necessary.

## Norwegian-language conventions
- Language: set proofing language to Norwegian Bokmål (nb-NO) (or Nynorsk nn-NO if the audience uses it); keep consistent. Don't mix English terms unnecessarily; use established loanwords where natural.
- Capitalisation: Norwegian uses sentence case for titles ("Slik fungerer løsningen"), not Title Case. Only the first word and proper nouns are capitalised. Months and weekdays are lowercase (mandag, januar).
- Numbers: decimal comma (3,5), space as thousands separator, preferably non-breaking (1 250 000). Space before % and units (12 %, 5 kg, 20 °C). Dates: "25. november 2026" or 25.11.2026. Time with colon or period: kl. 14:30 / 14.30. Currency: "kr 1 500" or "1 500 kr"; "mill."/"mrd." (not "M"/"B"). Chart axes must follow the same decimal comma format (set locale/number format).
- Punctuation: use «guillemets» for quotes (or „…“); en dash (–) for ranges and as parenthetical dash with spaces; avoid periods at the end of titles and bullet fragments; be consistent for full-sentence bullets.
- Compound words are written together (samarbeidspartner, ikke "samarbeids partner" – "særskriving" is a common error that changes meaning). Check hyphenation in acronyms: "PDF-fil", "EU-land", "2020-tallet".
- Æ, ø, å must render: choose fonts with full Norwegian glyph support and test after PDF export.
- Tone: concise and active; avoid nominalisation and long compound strings. Norwegian text is ~10–20 % longer than English: allow room, and test long words in narrow columns (turn hyphenation off, rephrase instead).
- Follow the organisation's profile (Språkrådet guidance: klarspråk) and Universell utforming: contrast, alt text, reading order, and captions for video. The legal requirements (forskrift om universell utforming av IKT-løsninger, lovdata.no/dokument/SF/forskrift/2013-06-21-732) apply to web solutions: private ones WCAG 2.0 A/AA (except 1.2.3, 1.2.4, 1.2.5), public ones EN 301 549 v3.2.1 (§ 4b). Slide decks are not automatically covered, but published decks (e.g. on a website) may be; WCAG is a good target regardless.

## Checklist before delivering
1. Every slide has one takeaway title; no slide has > ~30 words.
2. Body ≥ 24 pt, nothing < 18 pt (sources ≥ 12 pt).
3. ≤ 2 fonts, ≤ 5 colors, contrast ≥ 4.5:1.
4. Margins ≥ 0.5 in, elements aligned to grid, ≥ 40 % whitespace.
5. Charts titled with insight, zero-based bars, labelled directly, sourced.
6. Images sharp, uncropped distortion-free, alt text added.
7. Norwegian: sentence case, decimal comma, nb-NO proofing, no særskriving.
8. Run through slide-sorter view: the deck should look consistent and the story should read from titles alone.
