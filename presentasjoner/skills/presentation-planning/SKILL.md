# Presentation planning

Plan on paper first; open PowerPoint last. Reply in the user's language (Norwegian or English) and match the language of the existing deck.

## 1. Clarify (ask only what is missing; otherwise assume and state assumptions)
- **Audience**: who, how many, prior knowledge, what they care about, what they fear/resist, decision-makers present?
- **Purpose**: inform, persuade, decide, teach, or inspire? What should the audience *know, feel, do* afterwards?
- **Context**: time slot (incl. Q&A), room/online, projector or handout, language, follow-up material.
- **One core message**: a single sentence a listener could repeat ("Big Idea": a point of view + why it matters to them).

## 2. Pick a structure
| Structure | Use when | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| Minto pyramid (answer first) | Executives, decisions, status, time-poor | Answer/recommendation → 2-4 supporting arguments (MECE) → evidence for each |
| Problem → solution (SCQA: Situation, Complication, Question, Answer) | Proposals, pitches, change | Context → what is wrong/at stake → options → recommendation → next steps |
| Duarte sparkline | Inspiring, vision, keynotes | Alternate "what is" and "what could be", build tension, end on a call to action and the "new bliss" |
| Chronological / how-to | Training, walkthroughs, demos | Steps in order, recap at end |
| Before–after–bridge | Short persuasive pitches | Today's pain → desired state → how to get there |

Default: Minto for business audiences, sparkline for talks meant to move people, how-to for teaching.

## 3. Outline
- Write slide titles as **full-sentence takeaways** (the title is the claim; the slide body is the proof). Reading only titles should tell the story.
- Opening: hook (question, surprising number, short story), why it matters to *them*, roadmap (≤3 items).
- Body: 3 main points (max ~5). One idea per slide. Visual/evidence per slide noted in outline (chart, image, diagram, quote).
- Closing: summary of the message, clear ask/next step, final memorable line. Don't end on "Questions?" or a Q&A slide alone.
- Put detail in an **appendix** rather than on main slides.
- Suggested outline format per slide: `# | Title (claim) | Visual/evidence | Time | Key speaker point`.

## 4. Timing
- Plan time per idea, not per slide: complex ideas may span several slides (TED guidance). Rehearse against the time budget; ~1-2 min per content slide is only a starting estimate.
- Budget: opening ~10%, body ~75-80%, close ~10-15%. Reserve 10-20% of the slot for Q&A and buffer; plan to finish early.
- Allocate time per section first, then per slide. Mark must-keep vs. cut-if-late slides.
- Rehearse out loud with a timer; adjust the outline, not the speaking speed.

## 5. Speaker notes
- Write what you will *say*, in spoken language: short sentences, key phrases, not a script to read.
- Per slide: the point in one sentence, transition to the next slide, any story/number/source to mention, planned time.
- Mark cues (click/animation, demo, pause, question to audience).
- Handouts: give a separate handout rather than packing slides with detail. Plan a closing statement after Q&A. Full scripts in notes are fine if you rehearse; otherwise use concise cues.
- Keep slides minimal so the notes carry the detail; handouts/appendix carry the rest.

## 6. Checklist before delivering the outline
- Core message in one sentence? Every slide supports it, otherwise cut or move to appendix.
- Audience's "so what?" answered early?
- Titles tell the story alone? One idea per slide?
- Times sum to the slot minus buffer?
- Clear call to action at the end?
